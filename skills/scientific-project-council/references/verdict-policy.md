# Verdict Policy and Decision Gates

| Verdict | Meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| APPROVE | Scientifically meaningful, measurable, feasible, and adequately scoped; required outcomes are addressed; implementation can start. Ordinary manageable execution risks may remain, but no unresolved critical blocker. |
| REFINE | Direction is viable; fix specific scientific weaknesses before serious implementation, such as metrics, baseline, contribution definition, or course integration. |
| RESCOPE | Central idea has value but exceeds credible time/resources or has avoidable complexity/dependency risk; reduce it. Prefer this over rejection when a smaller academically meaningful core survives. |
| RETHINK | Fundamental direction or course fit is weak; redesign the concept before continuing. |
| REJECT | Current direction should not be pursued: no defensible academic value, no feasible meaningful core under fixed constraints, an inherently unmeasurable objective, irreparable required-course mismatch, or an essentially identical copy with no valid replication/learning contribution under the rubric. |

Assess scientific value, novelty, feasibility, course coverage, measurability, and demo potential separately. Prior-art classification is not the NOVELTY rating. `NOT REQUIRED` applies to NOVELTY only when justified by the academic objective; it never excuses weak work. The verdict responds to the decisive blocker rather than an average of ratings.

Never accept APPROVE with LOW feasibility, an unanswered supported critical objection, absent baseline, no measurable experiment, an indispensable unverified resource treated as available, or a still-oversized MVP. UNKNOWN essentials prevent unconditional approval when the conclusion depends on them. Use a suitable non-approval verdict plus the needed validation, not fabricated certainty or a blanket rejection for missing paperwork.

For required courses, no unexplained `MISSING` coverage is acceptable in any ruling. APPROVE additionally requires that no genuinely required outcome remains missing. An explicit professor-approved waiver can remove an outcome from the required set; report the waiver instead of silently counting it as coverage. For PBL, superficial integration may require REFINE or RETHINK even if the system is technically impressive.
